\section*{Executive summary}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
This report documents a pipeline that reads silicone-elastomer formulations
and their compression-set measurements out of patents and journal articles,
the frozen dataset it currently produces, and the predictive baseline that
dataset supports. The work targets a practical question for seal design.
Given a documented recipe (base polysiloxane, filler, crosslinker, catalyst
or initiator, additives) and a test protocol, can compression set be
predicted without running the test?

The published record is large but almost entirely qualitative. We began with
\nCorpusDocs{} downloaded PDFs. Of these, \nPropDocs{} yield at least one
extracted property. Only \nCsDocs{} report any compression-set value at all,
and once a value is required to sit next to a stated composition and a
complete test protocol (temperature, time, deflection), the population
shrinks to \nCsCondDocs{} documents. The clean release \release{} holds
\nRelRows{} measurements over \nRelForms{} formulations from \nRelDocs{}
documents (\nRelPatents{} patents, \nRelJournals{} journal articles). Every
value in it was read from a page image by a locally served vision-language
model, resolved to a canonical substance and role by a reviewed language-model
prompt, checked against a verbatim source quote, and rebuilt from inputs
rather than patched in place. A two-reader audit of a stratified sample of released rows
against their source pages found the released compression-set value correct
in 96.8\% of checkable cases (Wilson 95\% interval 94.7--98.1\%), with
temperature and deflection correct in every checked case.

On this release, test conditions alone (deflection, time, temperature) give a
leave-one-family-out $R^2$ of \nLofoConditions{} with a mean absolute error
of \nLofoMaeConditions{} percentage points. Adding any of four chemistry
representations (stated viscosity, ingredient-text embeddings, a blend of
text and parsed descriptors, ingredient categories) changes that score by
between \nDeltaDescriptorsVisc{} and \nDeltaEmbWeighted{}, and every one of
those intervals includes zero. At 67 documents the smallest gain a paired
test could distinguish from zero lies between \nSpgMin{} and \nSpgMax{}
$R^2$, larger than any chemistry effect observed. The corpus therefore
supports a conditions-only baseline and a documented dataset, and it does not
yet support any claim that composition features improve transfer to unseen
patent families. Random-row cross-validation on the same rows reaches
$R^2$ of \nRandomCategories{}, which measures interpolation within known
documents and is reported only as a diagnostic.

Two items are requested from collaborators. First, a small set of
formulations with trusted chemistry and test conditions that could serve as a
fixed validation set. Second, a statement of the prediction error that would
change a design decision, so that the corpus size needed to prove a useful
model can be estimated rather than assumed.

\section{Purpose and scope}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Why compression set}
Silicone elastomers combine thermal stability, chemical inertness, and
elastic recovery, which is why they dominate seals that must hold a load for
years \citep{mazurek2019silicone,patel2001thermal}. Compression set is the
fraction of an imposed deflection that a specimen fails to recover after a
stated time at a stated temperature \citep{astmd395}. It is the single
property most directly tied to seal life, and it is also the property whose
value depends most on how the test was run. A button held 22~h at
175\degC{} under 25\% deflection and a button held 70~h at 150\degC{} are
different experiments, and a value reported without those three numbers cannot
be compared to anything. The data model in this project therefore treats the
test protocol as part of the measurement rather than as metadata.

The property depends on network chemistry as much as on protocol. Residual
set arises from chain slippage at filler surfaces, from post-cure
crosslinking under the test load, and from scission and re-formation of
siloxane bonds at temperature, so the base polymer's end groups and vinyl
content, the crosslinker's Si--H content, the filler's surface area and
treatment, and the choice of an addition, condensation, or peroxide cure all
enter \citep{mazurek2019silicone,yue2013tensile,patel2004stress}. A useful
dataset must carry that chemistry in a form a model can read.

The broader difficulty is well documented. Polymer formulations resist
data-driven modeling because the design space is large and much of the
practical knowledge is recorded qualitatively
\citep{audus2017polymer,butler2018machine,ramprasad2017machine}, and the
experimental record that could anchor models is dispersed across patents and
articles with incompatible layouts, trade-name opacity, and reprinted data
\citep{olivetti2020data,hiszpanski2020nanomaterial}. Curated polymer databases
such as PoLyInfo \citep{otsuka2011polyinfo} cover homopolymer properties and
not formulated elastomers, and the polymer language models built on them
\citep{kuenneth2021polymer,kuenneth2023polybert,xu2023transpolymer} take a
repeat-unit string as input, which a filled, catalyzed, post-cured network
does not have. Text-mined materials databases have been built for inorganic
synthesis recipes, solar-cell devices, and magnetic transition temperatures
\citep{kononova2019text,kim2017machine,beard2019perovskite,court2018magnetic},
first with rule-based toolkits \citep{swain2016chemdataextractor} and more
recently with large language models prompted to return structured records
\citep{dagdelen2024structured,polak2024extracting,zheng2023chatgpt,xie2024creation,schilling2025text}.
Those efforts read text. Formulation tables in patents are laid out
visually, span pages, and rotate, which is why this project reads rendered
pages with a vision-language model instead.

\subsection{The state of the published record}
Figure~\ref{fig:problem} surveys the record from which the dataset is built.
Panel (a) is a funnel over downloaded PDFs. The starting set contains
\nCorpusDocs{} PDFs; \nPropDocs{} yield at least one extracted property of any kind;
\nCsDocs{} report a compression-set value (\nCsRows{} raw rows);
\nCsCompDocs{} attach such a value to a stated composition; \nCsCondDocs{}
also state the full test protocol; and \nRelDocs{} survive the remaining
checks into the clean release. Each step in that funnel is a source that
describes a material qualitatively without giving the numbers needed to use
it quantitatively.

Panel (b) shows where the numbers go missing. Of \nCsPopulation{}
compression-set rows that are neither prophetic nor out of range,
\nRowValuePct\% carry a value in the row and \nRowIngredientsPct\% sit next
to a composition, but the row itself states a test temperature for only
\nRowTempPct\%, a time for \nRowTimePct\%, and a deflection for
\nRowDeflectionPct\%. All fields appear together in the row for
\nRowAllPct\% of measurements. The protocol is usually written once, in a
methods paragraph pages from the results table, and sometimes only as the
name of a standard. Reading the whole document and resolving those statements
back onto each row raises deflection coverage to \nResDeflectionPct\%,
temperature to \nResTempPct\%, and full specification to \nResAllPct\%.
Panel (c) shows the naming problem. Within the released formulations alone,
the role \nSpellTopRole{} appears under \nSpellTopCount{} distinct printed
strings, ranging from IUPAC-style names through trade codes such as
``KE-1950-50'' to anonymous labels such as ``component (A)''. The pipeline's
job is to turn that record into rows in which chemistry, amount, protocol, and
value are all present and all traceable to a page.

\section{The pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
% ===========================================================================
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} lays out the pipeline in three lanes. The first
finds and screens documents, the second reads them into candidate records,
and the third rebuilds a database from those records, freezes a release, and
evaluates models on it. Two conventions run through all three. Every document
carries a permanent opaque identifier assigned at first sighting, so richer
metadata learned later never renames it. And every data file is regenerated by
rerunning the stage that produces it. When a value is wrong, the fix goes into
the stage or into a reviewed correction file, and the stage reruns; no
released CSV is edited by hand.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pipeline_from_source_discovery_to_grouped_evaluation.png}
  \caption{\textbf{From source discovery to grouped evaluation.} Lane 1
  renders one query specification against every source, assigns a permanent
  document identity, parses each PDF to text once, and applies a two-stage
  language-model screen. Lane 2 reads page images into schema-enforced
  records, resolves ingredient identity, role, and test conditions with
  reviewed prompts, checks each value against a verbatim quote, and applies
  curated corrections. Lane 3 rebuilds the database, freezes a release,
  builds feature arms, and evaluates with patent families held out. Audit
  findings from lane 3 return to lane 2 as fixes to a stage, never as edits
  to a release.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Discovery and acquisition}
Documents come from five sources. Patents are harvested from the USPTO Open
Data Portal and from the European Patent Office's Open Patent Services
(EP and WO publications); journal articles are located through OpenAlex,
Semantic Scholar, and the OSTI grey-literature index. A single declarative
specification of material synonyms, form terms, and test standards renders
the query for every source, so a synonym added once reaches every source's
next harvest. The specification deliberately omits the property from the
query. Conjoining ``compression set'' with the material terms collapsed USPTO
recall almost to zero, because patents state the property in an example table
rather than in the title or abstract, and in a screening pilot on OSTI grey
literature only 2 of 76 candidates showed compression-set evidence in the
abstract against 8 of 14 when the full text was fetched. The property
judgment is deferred to a full-text screen.

Duplicates are removed by identity rather than by text. A DOCDB patent
family identifier, a DOI, a content hash, and provider identifiers are
appended to an identity ledger under the permanent document id, and a curated
subset of high-confidence identifiers merges duplicates automatically. This
is also the mechanism that recognizes an EP or WO family member as a reprint
of a US document already in hand and keeps only the fullest one. Family
identity matters again at evaluation time, because rows from two members of
one family are near-duplicates and must never be split across training and
test folds (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

Every downloaded PDF is transcribed to structured text by a vision-language
parser before any question is asked of it. Screening is then a
text-classification problem asked of a local language model in two stages.
The first asks whether the matrix polymer is a polysiloxane; only documents
that pass are asked whether they report quantitative compression-set
measurements. An audit mode reruns the second stage on a sample of
first-stage rejections to measure the recall the cascade costs. No pattern
matching enters either judgment; a keyword search is used only to select
which passages of a long document are shown to the model. We began with
\nCorpusDocs{} downloaded PDFs: 314 journal articles and 2{,}611 patents.
The silicone-matrix screen retains 2{,}409 documents. Of those, 165 (54
journal articles and 111 patents) also report quantitative compression-set
measurements.

\subsection{Page-image extraction}
Each PDF page is rendered to an image and passed to a vision-language model
(Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct \citep{qwen3_2025,qwen25vl2025}, served locally
with vLLM \citep{kwon2023efficient} on an NVIDIA H200) with a reviewed prompt
and a JSON schema. The model returns, for each formulation on
the page, every ingredient's verbatim name, role text, amount, and unit, the
processing conditions, and the measured properties with their printed
conditions. Values are captured as printed. No unit conversion, synonym
substitution, or inference from a trade name happens at this stage, so the
record can later be checked cell by cell against the page it came from. The
record also flags prophetic examples, keeps the page number and table label,
and records the model's own confidence. Reading the image rather than
extracted text matters for patents, where a composition table often spans
pages, rotates, or repeats a header, and where the text layer of a scanned
document is unreliable; the same argument motivated OCR-free document
understanding models \citep{kim2022ocrfree,blecher2023nougat}.

An independent text-based path (a commercial document parser feeding the same
schema through a text-only model) runs alongside the image path on every
document. Neither dominates. Comparing the two per document catches merged
or flattened tables that either one alone would pass silently, and the
disagreement census is one of the pipeline's standing quality checks. The
release described here uses the image path for every document.

\subsection{Resolution of ingredients, roles, and conditions}
A printed ingredient string becomes usable through three resolutions, each
performed by a local language model under a prompt that was reviewed before
it ran, each cached with the model identifier and prompt hash, and each
applied only after its output has been checked against source quotes.

Identity resolution maps the verbatim string to a canonical substance and,
where the document defines it, to a CAS number or structure. Trade codes and
anonymous labels are resolved from the document's own glossary sentences
(``component (A) is a dimethylvinylsiloxy-terminated polydimethylsiloxane
having a viscosity of 10{,}000 mPa$\cdot$s''), and the descriptive paragraph
itself is kept as the canonical text because it carries the chemistry a model
can use. Role resolution assigns each ingredient one or more labels from a
reviewed taxonomy of 41 leaves under 16 parents (base polymer, crosslinker,
cure catalyst, cure initiator, cure inhibitor, reinforcing filler, extending
filler, filler treating agent, and so on). The distinction between catalyst
and initiator is chemical rather than nominal. Platinum, tin, and titanium
compounds in addition and condensation cures are regenerated and are
catalysts; peroxides and azo compounds are consumed and are initiators, even
when the source calls its peroxide a ``curing catalyst''. No regular
expression is used for any chemical classification; the taxonomy and a
reviewed prompt are the only classifier.

Condition resolution recovers the test protocol from wherever the document
states it. A string-level pass reads the condition text printed beside a
value; a document-level pass reads the methods text and the standard named,
distinguishes a test hold from a pre-aging exposure, and attaches
temperature, time, deflection, and medium to each measurement with a
verbatim quote as evidence. Each resolved field carries its source
(row text, methods statement, human verification, or explicit assumption) so
a downstream user can filter on provenance.

\subsection{Verification and correction}
Agent output is a candidate until it has been checked against the source and
promoted, and a candidate is never written into a curated file. Verification
takes three forms. Every resolved value must be accompanied by a verbatim
quote from its own document, and quotes that cannot be located on the cited
page fail the check. A sample of released rows is re-read from the page
images by two independent readers and compared cell by cell
(Section~\ref{sec:accuracy}). Compositions that collapse to identical
ingredient sets with different measured values are treated as detectors of
dropped variables or parser failures and are sent back for repair rather than
tolerated as a dataset ceiling.

Corrections are centralized in a set of schema-validated correction files
(curated formulation components, curated conditions, glossary overrides,
amount-basis overrides, translations). A human correction beats an agent
correction, which beats the raw extraction. Each correction row carries its
source, reviewer, and date. Documents in which silicone is an additive or
crosslinker in a non-silicone matrix (polyurethane, thermoplastic
vulcanizate, styrenic block copolymer) are out of scope and are quarantined
through a script that archives rather than deletes.

\subsection{Rebuild and freeze}
Normalized records, the corpus index, the resolution caches, and the
correction files are loaded into a single DuckDB store by one build script.
The build is a fixed point of its inputs, verified empirically by two
consecutive rebuilds that produced bit-identical content for every table
except the one that records the build's own git commit. A release is a frozen
snapshot of that store passed through an admission rule (usable,
non-prophetic, in physical range, numeric target, stated composition, all
three test conditions present, recipe complete enough to model) together with
a manifest that pins the cohort file, the database build stamp, an archive of
the machine caches, and the SHA-256 of every correction file. The modeling
code reads only the frozen snapshot, never the live database.

\section{Data model and conventions}
\label{sec:datamodel}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Formulation composition}
A formulation is a list of ingredient rows. Each row carries the verbatim
name, the resolved canonical substance, the role from the taxonomy, the
amount with its basis (parts per hundred of base polymer, weight percent of
the final formulation, grams, ppm), and any characterization the source
states (viscosity, molecular weight, vinyl or Si--H content, BET surface
area, particle size, surface treatment). The amount basis is recorded per
row and summarized per formulation, because a recipe that mixes bases cannot
be converted to fractions without an assumption the source did not make.
Patent examples that describe multi-part systems (Part A and Part B, or
liquids I, II, and III) are split into per-part formulations, since a
half-recipe that cannot cure on its own would otherwise be modeled as if it
were a complete material.

\subsection{The compression-set measurement}
Each measurement is one row. It carries the value in percent, the test
temperature, the test time, the applied deflection, the medium (air, a named
oil, water, or humid air), the specimen geometry where stated (disc or
O-ring, thickness, diameter), the test standard where named, and any post-cure
stated for that specimen. An aging series is several rows, one per protocol,
and is never collapsed to one. Where the source distinguishes a pre-aging
exposure from the test hold, both are kept as separate fields; the two are
different experiments, and the pipeline is built so that a mirror of one into
the other cannot manufacture agreement.

\subsection{Not stated versus not applicable}
An empty cell in the release means the source does not state the value. A
field that does not apply to a row is never left empty silently. The clean
release tables carry only fields that apply to every row; fields that apply
to some rows (post-cure, pre-aging) live in the provenance tables with an
explicit flag. Provenance is tracked at the field level. A measurement's
temperature can come from the row text, from a methods statement, from a
human verification, or from an explicit assumption, and the release records
which. A user who wants only human-verified protocols can filter for them.

\subsection{Scope}
The corpus keeps only materials whose matrix is a polysiloxane. Blends in
which a silicone is the minor phase, a crosslinker, or a processing aid are
excluded, as are epoxy, polyurethane, and thermoplastic-vulcanizate systems
that merely contain a siloxane. The screen is applied by a language model
reading the document, and its rejections are audited. One journal article on
a DGEBA epoxy modified with a polydimethylsiloxane was retrieved by the
material query, passed the keyword filter, and was excluded only by the
matrix screen; it is the canonical example of why the screen reads the
document rather than the abstract.

\section{Dataset release \release}
\label{sec:release}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Contents}
Release \release{} was frozen on 13 September 2026 from a database build of
the same day. It holds \nRelRows{} compression-set measurements over
\nRelForms{} formulations from \nRelDocs{} documents (\nRelPatents{}
patents and \nRelJournals{} journal articles published between \nRelYearMin{}
and \nRelYearMax), \nRelIngredientRows{} ingredient rows, and
\nRelDistinctIngredients{} distinct ingredient strings, every one of them in
English and judged either chemically specific or a typed generic (``fumed
silica'' passes; ``component E2'' does not). No formulation and no measurement
was held back at export; earlier releases had held back rows whose
ingredients could not be named, and the glossary and translation work that
closed that gap is part of what separates \release{} from its predecessors.

Figure~\ref{fig:dataset} summarizes the release. The compression-set values
have median \nRelMedian\% and interquartile range \nRelIQRlo--\nRelIQRhi\%,
with a long tail to 100\% that comes mostly from aged specimens and
comparative examples (panel a). Measurements are unevenly distributed across
documents, with a median of \nDocMedianRows{} per document and a maximum of
\nDocMaxRows{} (panel b); this concentration is the reason evaluation must
hold out whole documents. Amount bases are mixed (panel c): \nBasisPartsPerHundred{}
formulations are in parts per hundred, \nBasisWeightPct{} in weight percent,
\nBasisGrams{} in grams, and \nBasisMixed{} mix bases within one recipe. A
base polymer is named in \nRoleBasePolymerPct\% of formulations, a
crosslinker in \nRoleCrosslinkerPct\%, a cure catalyst in
\nRoleCatalystPct\%, and a cure initiator in \nRoleInitiatorPct\% (panel d);
the two cure agents are close to mutually exclusive, as the chemistry
requires. The \nProtocolDistinct{} distinct test protocols cluster on the
standard industrial holds of 22~h at 175\degC{} (\nProtocolTwentyTwoHOneSevenFive{}
measurements) and 70~h at 150\degC{} (\nProtocolSeventyHOneFifty{}
measurements) but extend from room temperature to above 200\degC{} and from
hours to thousands of hours (panel e). Panel (f) records what the sources
state. Temperature, time, and deflection are present on every released row
by construction. Medium is filled for every row (air unless the source
names another medium), specimen shape is stated for
\nStatedSpecimenShape\%, specimen thickness for \nStatedSpecimenThickness\%,
and a test standard for \nStatedTestStandard\%. Those gaps are not filled;
they are the record.

\subsection{Accuracy of the release}
\label{sec:accuracy}
Sixty released measurements were sampled, stratified by extraction path,
script, and source type, and each was traced to its source page. A reader
transcribed the page image, compared each parser's cell output against the
transcription, and checked the released row; seven pages received a second,
blind read. Of 437 checkable compression-set values the released value was
correct for 423 (96.8\%, Wilson 95\% interval 94.7--98.1\%). Temperature was
correct in every case, deflection in every case, and time in 99.7\%.
Ingredient amounts were correct in 97.0\% of 332 checkable cells [94.5, 98.4]
and the amount basis in every case. Inter-reader agreement on the seven
double-read pages was 81\%. At the parser level, the image-based path
reproduced 85.6\% of table cells [84.4, 86.7] and the text-based path 75.8\%
[74.1, 77.5]; the difference is dominated by merged or flattened tables and
by rows shifted across columns.

The audit produced a short list of confirmed errors that remain in
\release{} and will be fixed in the next freeze through the correction files
rather than by editing the release. Nine formulations in one US patent
carry values from a neighbouring table under the wrong condition (released
14--55\% against printed 71--98\%). One amount in another US patent is the
neighbouring row's value (1.54 against printed 1.15). One inhibitor amount
was read as 0.4 against a printed 0.04, and one time as 1{,}008~h against a
printed 1{,}000~h. Each is listed with its source page in the audit summary
so that a user of \release{} can exclude the affected rows.

\subsection{Release history}
Releases are immutable and named. The first pinned cohort after the
adversarial audit of machine-reconstructed tables held 796 rows from 45
documents. Release cs\_v11 held 873 rows from 65 documents; cs\_v12 (2
September 2026) held 856 rows, 447 formulations, and 58 documents after five
patent-family reprint pairs were quarantined; cs\_v13 (11 September) held
1{,}043 rows, 593 formulations, and 67 documents; \release{} (13 September)
holds \nRelRows{}, \nRelForms{}, and \nRelDocs. Between cs\_v13 and
\release{} two documents were quarantined (one out of scope, one with a
defective extraction) and one was added. Numbers reported at earlier meetings
belong to the release they were computed on and are not comparable across
releases without that qualification.

\section{A predictive baseline}
\label{sec:model}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Target and feature arms}
The target is the compression-set value of each row. The conditions floor
uses four columns derived from the three required conditions: deflection,
$T/1000$ in kelvin, $\log_{10} t$ in hours, and their product $T\log_{10}t/1000$.
The last three span the Larson--Miller family of time-temperature
parameters, so the collapse constant is fitted rather than assumed. Every
other arm appends one block of chemistry to those four columns, and every
arm is evaluated on identical rows and folds, so each score is a paired
change against the same floor.

Four chemistry blocks are compared. \emph{Stated viscosity} is the base
polymer's viscosity where the source gives it, with a flag for ``not
stated''; it is the one numeric descriptor with enough coverage to test
alone. \emph{Ingredient-text embeddings} encode each ingredient's resolved
description with a materials-science text encoder (MatSciBERT
\citep{gupta2022matscibert}), pool the vectors within a formulation weighted
by amount, and reduce them inside each training fold. \emph{Text and descriptors} is a prediction-level blend, with
fixed equal weights, of the embedding arm and an arm of parsed physical
descriptors (viscosity, molecular weight, vinyl and Si--H content, filler
surface area, particle size, surface treatment) that records only values the
source states. \emph{Ingredient categories} is the presence and
amount-weighted share of each taxonomy role in the formulation. Ridge
regression carries the floor; gradient-boosted trees
\citep{friedman2001greedy,chen2016xgboost}, with preprocessing in
scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011scikit}, carry the chemistry arms, and the tree estimator's own conditions-only score is
reported so that the paired gain is always measured against the same
estimator.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
Rows from one patent family are near-duplicates, and random splitting of
such data leaks the identity of the source into the test fold
\citep{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage,roberts2017cross}. Every
evaluation therefore groups by DOCDB family, with the document as the group
where no family tag exists;
the \nRelDocs{} documents form \nRelGroups{} groups. Three regimes are
reported and named consistently. \emph{Grouped five-fold} holds out whole
families and averages over \nLadderRepeats{} fold-assignment repeats; the
reported spread is fold noise, not an effect size. \emph{Leave one family
out} holds out each group in turn and pools the predictions; it is the
regime used for the paired comparison. \emph{Random rows} splits individual
measurements and is an in-distribution diagnostic only, since siblings from
one table sit on both sides of the split. The distinction between
interpolation within known sources and extrapolation to new ones is the same
one drawn for materials discovery benchmarks
\citep{meredig2018can,dunn2020benchmarking,wang2020machine}. The gain of each chemistry arm is
its leave-one-family-out $R^2$ minus the same estimator's conditions floor.
Its 95\% interval comes from recomputing the gain while leaving out one
document family at a time, and the \emph{smallest provable gain} is the gain
that a paired test could distinguish from zero at this corpus size.
Scaling, imputation, and dimensionality reduction are fitted inside each
training fold.

\subsection{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} and Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} give the ladder. The
conditions floor reaches a leave-one-family-out $R^2$ of \nLofoConditions{}
(MAE \nLofoMaeConditions{} points) and a grouped $R^2$ of
\nGroupedConditions. The text-embedding arm reaches \nLofoEmbWeighted{}
and the blend \nLofoBlendEmbdescFifty{}, each within a few hundredths of
the floor; stated viscosity and ingredient categories fall below it. Against
the tree estimator's own floor (\nLofoXgbConditions), the paired gains are
\nDeltaDescriptorsVisc{} for viscosity, \nDeltaEmbWeighted{} for text,
\nDeltaBlendEmbdescFifty{} for the blend, and \nDeltaCategories{} for
categories, with 95\% intervals of [\nCiLoEmbWeighted, \nCiHiEmbWeighted]
for the text arm and [\nCiLoBlendEmbdescFifty, \nCiHiBlendEmbdescFifty] for
the blend. Every interval includes zero. The smallest provable gain at 67
documents is \nSpgDescriptorsVisc{} for the viscosity arm and between
\nSpgBlendEmbdescFifty{} and \nSpgEmbWeighted{} for the higher-dimensional
arms,
which is larger than any gain observed.

Random-row scores on the same rows are \nRandomConditions{} for conditions
alone and \nRandomCategories{} for categories. The gap between those numbers
and the grouped scores is the size of the document fingerprint. A model that
has seen a patent's other examples can predict the next one; a model that has
not seen the patent gains little from knowing its chemistry, at least in the
representations tried and at this corpus size.

\subsection{What the result does and does not say}
The result bounds what the present corpus can support. It does not say that
composition is irrelevant to compression set, which would contradict both the
chemistry and the within-document variation visible in the data. It says that
at 67 source families, with chemistry represented as stated viscosity, text
embeddings, parsed descriptors, or taxonomy categories, the transfer gain
from chemistry is smaller than the gain the corpus could prove. Four
independent increases in corpus size over the project's history (a doubling
of documents, an EPO harvest, a 20\% increase in complete recipe-property
pairs, and the present clean release) each left the grouped score near the
floor, so document count alone is not the lever. A rough scaling of the
provable gain suggests that proving a gain of 0.05 would need on the order of
130 well-documented source families. The alternative lever is a fixed,
trusted validation set on which representation choices can be compared
without the noise of unseen-document transfer, which is the request made in
Section~\ref{sec:next}. Text embeddings of ingredient descriptions were tried
because unsupervised embeddings have been shown to carry materials knowledge
from the literature \citep{tshitoyan2019unsupervised}, and because recent
polymer models pool language-model embeddings of constituent descriptions in
the same way \citep{zhang2025polyllmem}; three encoders (a general sentence
encoder \citep{reimers2019sentencebert}, MatSciBERT, and a large
general-purpose embedding model) were compared under the same paired harness
in earlier releases and MatSciBERT was retained.

One caveat applies to the text arm as it was run. A check made while
preparing this report found that the frozen embedding block carries a zero
vector for \nEmbZeroForms{} of the \nEmbForms{} formulations and only
\nEmbDistinct{} distinct vectors in all, because the embedding cache had been
built on an earlier generation of ingredient strings and \release{} renamed
most of them (89 of \nEmbStrings{} strings had a cached vector when the
check was made on 16 September 2026, before the cache was refreshed). The text arm in Table~\ref{tab:ladder} therefore saw no
chemistry at all for three formulations in ten and the same vector for many
of the rest, and its score is a lower bound on what the representation can
do. The cache has been refreshed for
the \release{} strings and the ladder will be rerun; the conditions floor
and the viscosity and category arms are unaffected.

\section{Lessons from verification}
\label{sec:lessons}
% ===========================================================================
A handful of cases changed the pipeline's design and are worth recording,
because each is a failure mode that a reader of the release should know was
looked for.

A correct value can be attached to the wrong chemistry. Two reprints of one
patent family (an EP and a US member) agreed on 27 of 27 compression-set
values and on 0 of 9 ingredient sets, because the two extractions had read
different tables as the composition. Values reproduce across duplicate
extractions; recipes do not. That finding is why every composition in the
release traces to a rendered page and why the duplicate-family census is
part of every freeze.

A model can invent a plausible chemical name. One extraction returned
``vinyltrimethylsiloxy-terminated'' where the source read
``vinyldimethylsiloxy-terminated''. The invented name is a valid
substituent pattern and would pass a chemistry-only check; only comparison to
the source catches it. Source checking and chemical plausibility are
complementary and neither replaces the other.

Machine reconstruction of complex tables was, at one stage, the dominant
source of defective compositions. An adversarial audit found that the admission
rule for reconstructed recipes had a precision of 26.9\%, and that 190 of
the 644 rows in the first pinned cohort rested on such reconstructions.
Those rows were re-transcribed from page images or quarantined, and the
current release admits no reconstruction that has not been checked against
the page.

Identical compositions with different measured values are a detector, not a
ceiling. In a census of such collisions 54\% were parser failures (a dropped
column, a merged row) and the rest were dropped variables such as a post-cure
or a different specimen. The collisions now route to a repair loop.

The document that most enriches the aging data carries no chemistry term in
its title. A seal-test survey with 63 compression-set rows would have been
lost to a title-only filter; it is the reason screening reads full text.

\section{Companion benchmark: tables from a 1968 reference volume}
\label{sec:noll}
% ===========================================================================
The table-reading tools were developed in parallel on a setting where every
answer can be checked. Noll's 1968 monograph on organosilicon chemistry
contains hundreds of pages of tabulated boiling points at stated pressures
for named compounds, many drawn as structures. It is scanned, some tables are
rotated, footnotes fall outside table crops, and the same compound appears
under several names, so it exercises every failure the patent corpus shows
while offering a fixed ground truth. A gold set was built by two blind
readers plus a parser, with reconciliation only on low-confidence
disagreements.

Two extraction arms were compared on matched pages. A fixed pipeline
(page screening, orientation detection, table region detection, vision
transcription, name-to-structure resolution) recovered 412 of 514 gold
structures exactly and 350 of 585 complete boiling-point and pressure pairs.
A tool-assisted arm, in which the model may zoom, rotate, separate a name
from a formula, or re-query a parser after a refusal, recovered 430 of 514
structures and 388 of 585 pairs on the same parser. The verified release
holds 509 measurements over 436 structures with 159 references, 73 of them
with a DOI. A Gaussian-process model on a 786-row modeling merge predicts
boiling point with a random-split test MAE of 15.7\degC{} and about 19\degC{}
under compound-grouped and scaffold-grouped splits, with calibrated
uncertainty. The grouping units there are molecules and Murcko scaffolds,
which are the molecular analogues of the patent family used in the silicone
work; the protocol is the same, the unit differs. The tools and the two-reader
gold protocol are the parts of that work that transfer to patents, and a
matched pilot on patent pages is the first item in Section~\ref{sec:next}.

\section{Next steps and requests to collaborators}
\label{sec:next}
% ===========================================================================
Three experiments are planned. The first is a matched pilot of the
tool-assisted reader on patent pages already in the release, scored on the
same audit sample as Section~\ref{sec:accuracy}, with correct complete
records per review hour as the metric. The second is adjudication of the
candidate polymer labels (end group, reactive-unit placement, backbone
monomers) that a whole-document pass has produced for 326 base polymers in 66
of the 67 release documents; 76 need review, and the labels enter the
feature set only after that review. The third is the next freeze, cs\_v15,
which will carry the audit's confirmed corrections and any new documents
that clear the screen.

Two inputs from collaborators would change what can be done. A small set of
formulations with chemistry and test conditions that Los Alamos trusts, with
or without measured values, would serve as a fixed validation set on which
representation choices can be compared without the noise of transfer to
unseen documents. And a statement of the prediction error that would change
a design decision, in percentage points of compression set at a named
protocol, would let the corpus size needed to prove a useful model be
estimated from the smallest-provable-gain curve rather than assumed. Which
silicone families (addition-cured liquid rubbers, peroxide-cured high
consistency rubbers, condensation-cured RTVs, foams) and which test regimes
matter most would set the priority for the next acquisition round.
